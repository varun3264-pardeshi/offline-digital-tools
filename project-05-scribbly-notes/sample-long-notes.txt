# CHAPTER 4: THERMODYNAMICS & HEAT TRANSFER

1. Fundamental Concepts
Thermodynamics is the branch of physics that deals with heat, work, and temperature, and their relation to energy, radiation, and physical properties of matter.

The four laws of thermodynamics govern the behavior of these quantities:
- Zeroth Law: If two systems are each in thermal equilibrium with a third, they are in thermal equilibrium with each other. This forms the basis for temperature measurement.
- First Law: Energy can neither be created nor destroyed; it can only change forms. Delta U = Q - W.
- Second Law: The total entropy of an isolated system always increases over time. Heat cannot spontaneously flow from a colder body to a hotter body.
- Third Law: As the temperature of a system approaches absolute zero (0 K), the entropy approaches a constant minimum.

2. Heat Engines & Carnot Cycle
A heat engine absorbs heat Q_H from a hot reservoir at temperature T_H, performs mechanical work W, and expels waste heat Q_C to a cold sink at temperature T_C.

Thermal efficiency is defined as:
Efficiency (eta) = Work Output / Heat Input = W / Q_H = 1 - (Q_C / Q_H).

The Carnot cycle represents an idealized, reversible thermodynamic cycle consisting of:
1. Reversible isothermal expansion (absorbing heat at T_H).
2. Reversible adiabatic expansion (temperature drops from T_H to T_C).
3. Reversible isothermal compression (expelling heat at T_C).
4. Reversible adiabatic compression (temperature rises from T_C to T_H).

Carnot efficiency limit:
eta_Carnot = 1 - (T_C / T_H).
No actual engine operating between two given reservoirs can be more efficient than a Carnot engine!

3. Entropy and Irreversibility
Entropy (S) is a measure of the molecular disorder or randomness in a physical system.
For any infinitesimal reversible process:
dS = dQ_rev / T.

In all spontaneous irreversible processes:
Delta S_universe = Delta S_system + Delta S_surroundings > 0.
This explains the "Arrow of Time" in classical physics: natural processes are unidirectional.

4. Heat Transfer Mechanisms
Conduction: Transfer of kinetic energy between adjacent molecules without bulk motion of matter.
Fourier's Law: q = -k * A * (dT / dx).

Convection: Heat transfer driven by macroscopic fluid movement.
Newton's Law of Cooling: q = h * A * (T_surface - T_fluid).

Radiation: Energy emitted via electromagnetic waves (photons). Requires no medium!
Stefan-Boltzmann Law: E = epsilon * sigma * A * T^4.

5. Key Takeaways & Exam Summary
- Remember that Kelvin must ALWAYS be used for temperature calculations in Carnot efficiency equations.
- In adiabatic processes, Q = 0, so Delta U = -W.
- In isothermal processes with ideal gases, Delta U = 0, so Q = W.
